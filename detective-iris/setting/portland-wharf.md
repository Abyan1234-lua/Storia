# SETTING — PORTLAND WHARF

---

## OVERVIEW

Portland Wharf is the working end of the Harbour District.

Where Callum Wharf Lane has cobblestones and amber streetlights and buildings that lean toward each other like old friends, Portland Wharf has cargo containers, diesel smell, and men who start their shifts at two in the morning without needing to be asked why.

It is not picturesque. It is functional in the way that matters — things move through here, things get loaded and unloaded, money changes hands in amounts that don't always appear in any ledger. The city needs Portland Wharf more than Portland Wharf needs the city to notice it.

---

## PHYSICAL DESCRIPTION

A working dock stretching roughly four hundred metres along the harbour's lower eastern edge. Three active berths capable of handling mid-size cargo vessels. A fourth berth at the far end that officially handles overflow and unofficially handles things people prefer not to document.

**The Warehouses:**
Six main warehouse structures running parallel to the water. Numbered, not named — W1 through W6. W1 through W4 are legitimate commercial storage. W5 is technically a cold storage facility but the temperature inside is never quite what the manifest says. W6 has changed hands four times in three years. Iris has a file on W6.

**The Night Market:**
An informal cluster of stalls and vans that assembles along the wharf road between ten at night and four in the morning. Food, hardware, information, things without obvious origin. Not illegal by any specific law. Not entirely comfortable either. Dock workers eat here. Night shift security eats here. Occasionally people who are not supposed to be in Velour City eat here and pretend they aren't.

**The Harbormaster's Office:**
A squat building at the wharf entrance that has been painted the same shade of municipal green since 1974. The current harbormaster has worked this wharf for twenty-one years, knows everything that moves through it, and has spoken to Iris on exactly three occasions — each one initiated by the harbormaster, each one brief, each one apparently worth Iris's full attention.

**The Crane:**
One large stationary crane at the W3 berth that has been out of service for eight months. Nobody has repaired it. Nobody has removed it. It is visible from most of the wharf and from certain windows in the Harbour District. At night, with the harbour fog, it looks like something that is paying attention.

---

## PEOPLE

Portland Wharf runs on a workforce that is almost entirely nocturnal by preference or necessity.

**Dock workers:** Pragmatic, territorial about their routines, deeply uninterested in strangers who ask questions. They know each other's business and nobody else's. Takumi will find them difficult to read. Iris finds them straightforward — people who want to be left alone are easy to negotiate with.

**Night Market regulars:** A more mixed population. Some are dock workers eating between shifts. Some are people from other parts of the city who come here because the Night Market doesn't ask what you're doing up at three in the morning. Some are people Iris recognizes and does not approach in front of Takumi.

**Security personnel:** Three rotating guards covering the warehouse row overnight. One of the night guards has an arrangement with Iris that involves him not seeing certain things in exchange for her not filing a specific report she has been holding for two years. This is understood between them without being discussed.

---

## SUPERNATURAL PRESENCE

Portland Wharf has a different supernatural texture than the rest of the Harbour District.

The older part of the Harbour District has spirits that are residual — emotional echoes, remnants of things that happened long ago. Portland Wharf has spirits that are *transactional*. Things pass through here, in both directions. The boundary between Velour City and what lies adjacent is not thin here the way it is at The Shelf — it is more like a door that gets opened and closed regularly by people who know where the handle is.

Iris monitors this differently than she monitors The Shelf. The Shelf is a natural phenomenon. Portland Wharf is infrastructure someone built and someone maintains and she has not yet identified who.

W6 is relevant to this. She has not told Takumi.

---

## RELEVANCE TO THE BLOOD BERYL CASE

The amulet entered Velour City through Portland Wharf.

Not through customs. Not through any documented channel. Through the fourth berth, on a vessel whose manifest listed ceramic kitchenware, on a night when the regular night guard was not on shift and the replacement filed a report that was technically complete and practically useless.

Iris knows this. She has not yet told Takumi how she knows this.

The Night Market vendor who facilitated the transfer has not been at their usual stall for eleven days. Their absence is the kind that suggests a decision rather than an accident.

---

## ATMOSPHERE

Portland Wharf should feel like:
- A place where the city's official story doesn't fully apply
- Functional and indifferent — it doesn't care about you, it cares about the work
- Quietly dangerous in a way that isn't theatrical — the danger here is the kind that comes from people protecting interests, not from malice for its own sake
- The kind of place where something ancient could arrive in a shipping container and nobody would find that surprising in retrospect

It should never feel:
- Cartoonishly criminal — it's grey, not black
- Abandoned or derelict — it's active, working, alive in its own rough way
- Disconnected from the rest of the Harbour District — Portland Wharf and Callum Wharf Lane are the same neighbourhood, just different ends of the same truth
