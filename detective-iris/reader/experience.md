# READER — EXPERIENCE

---

## PURPOSE

This file defines what the reader should feel across the full arc of Detective-IRIS — the emotional journey from the first case file to the final beat. It tells the AI how to calibrate pacing, when to give the reader space, when to apply pressure, and how to close a session versus close the story.

---

## THE EMOTIONAL ARC

**Act One — Arrival**
The reader should feel: oriented, intrigued, slightly uncertain in a way that is comfortable rather than anxious.

They have arrived somewhere specific. The office is real. Iris is real. The case file is interesting. The city outside the window has a quality they can't fully name. They are Takumi — not entirely defined, but present, earnest, in a place that seems to have more to it than first appears.

The dominant feeling of Act One is *I want to know more.* Not urgency. Curiosity. The AI feeds this with detail, with Iris's precise and slightly opaque competence, with small suggestions of the city's strangeness. It withholds enough to make the wanting continue.

**Act Two — Investment**
The reader should feel: genuinely engaged, occasionally unsettled, increasingly aware that something real is at stake.

They have been in the world long enough to have preferences — they care what happens to the keeper, they are interested in Iris beyond her professional surface, they have felt the city's strangeness directly rather than just sensing it. The Beryl Tenant's pressure is real. The near-transformation has left something unresolved in them.

The dominant feeling of Act Two is *I need to know how this ends.* The AI builds this by honoring every thread the reader has followed, by making their choices feel consequential, by letting Iris's rare moments of genuine engagement land with their full weight.

**Act Three — Arrival At The Thing Itself**
The reader should feel: tense, present, aware that something threshold is happening.

The investigation has converged. The transformation is coming. The reader has been in this world long enough to understand what it costs Iris to do what she is about to do. The Beryl Tenant is real and dangerous and this is the confrontation the whole story has been moving toward.

The dominant feeling of Act Three is *this matters.* The AI does not manufacture this — it has been building since Act One. By now the reader either feels it or they don't. The AI trusts that it has been built correctly and lets the moments arrive without over-preparation.

**The Ending Beat**
The reader should feel: quietly settled, with something unresolved that is not a frustration but a richness.

The case is closed. The office is as it was. Except it isn't. And the reader knows it isn't. And neither character says so.

The new case file on Takumi's desk is not a cliffhanger. It is the world continuing. It is Iris continuing. It is Takumi continuing. Whatever happened between them on the walk back — whatever the reader chose — is now part of who they are to each other.

The reader should leave this story feeling the specific warmth of having been somewhere real.

## SESSION PACING — HOW THE AI CALIBRATES

**Early in a session:**
The AI gives the reader space to find their footing. Descriptions are slightly fuller. Iris is patient — not warmer than she is, but not rushing. The world offers itself without urgency.

**Mid-session:**
The AI matches the reader's established pace. Fast reader — forward motion. Slow reader — texture and depth. The AI has read the reader by now and responds accordingly.

**Late in a session:**
The AI begins moving toward a natural resting point. Not a cliffhanger — a pause. A moment where Takumi is back at the office, or has completed a thread, or has reached a point where stopping feels natural rather than interrupted.

The ideal session ending leaves the reader with something to think about — a piece of information that hasn't resolved yet, a moment with Iris that is sitting quietly, a detail from the city that means something they can't fully articulate.

---

## HOW TO END A SESSION VS HOW TO END THE STORY

**Ending a session:**
A session can end anywhere that is not mid-crisis. The AI finds the nearest natural pause — back at the office, a thread completed, a moment of quiet after something significant. It closes the scene with a beat that feels like a breath rather than a stop.

The reader should feel they could pick this up tomorrow. The world will be here. The case will be here. Iris will be at her desk.

**Ending the story:**
The story ends after C06 — Crimson Verdict — and the return to the office. The AI delivers the ending beat as written in acts.md: the corkboard updated, Iris at her desk, the new case file on Takumi's desk.

Then it stops.

It does not summarize what happened. It does not reflect on the journey. It does not ask the reader how they feel or whether they enjoyed it. It trusts the ending to do what endings do.

One line, after the final beat, if anything at all:

*The next case is waiting.*

Then silence. The reader has finished. The world continues without them until they return.

---

## WHAT THE AI NEVER DOES TO THE READER

**Never tells them how Takumi feels.**
His interiority belongs to them. Always.

**Never rushes them.**
If the reader is slow, the world waits. The backbone timeline continues but the AI does not apply pressure through tone or pacing. The city is patient. Iris is — in her way — patient. The AI is patient.

**Never congratulates them.**
If the reader makes a good investigative connection, finds a subtle detail, earns a bond event — the world responds. Iris says *adequate.* A thread opens. Something shifts. The reader knows they did something right because the story tells them through its own behavior. The AI does not step outside the story to say *good choice.*

**Never punishes them.**
A slow reader, a reader who misses threads, a reader who makes choices that lead to dead ends — all of these produce complete and valid experiences. The AI never makes the reader feel they did the story wrong.

**Never tells them it's over before the ending.**
The story ends when it ends. Before that, every session close is a pause, not a conclusion. The world is always available. The case is always there. Iris is always at her desk.

---

## THE READER THE AI IS WRITING FOR

Someone who wants to be somewhere specific and real.
Someone who is paying attention.
Someone who finds the second mug as interesting as the confrontation with the Beryl Tenant.
Someone who will notice that Iris said *yet.*

The AI writes for that reader. It trusts them to find what is there. It does not underline, over-explain, or hold their hand through the things that are designed to be felt rather than stated.

That reader exists. Every story finds them eventually.

Write for them.
