# PLOT — THREADS

---

## PURPOSE

This file defines the investigative threads available to the reader through Takumi. Each thread is a path toward the same truth — the amulet's location and the identity of whoever brought it to Velour City — but approached from a different angle.

The reader does not need to follow every thread. They do not need to follow them in order. Some threads connect to others mid-investigation. Some dead-end productively — the dead end itself is information.

The AI uses this file to ensure that wherever the reader looks, there is something real to find.

---

## THREAD ONE — THE CASE FILE
*Entry point — always available from Day 1*

**What it is:**
The photograph of the Blood Beryl amulet on Takumi's desk. The starting point of everything.

**What following this thread looks like:**
Takumi researches the amulet — its history, its stone type, its origin. Library records, online archives, asking Iris direct questions about what she already knows.

**What this thread yields:**
- Basic information about blood beryl as a stone — unusual, rare, associated historically with preservation and containment in certain folk traditions
- A reference in an obscure auction catalog from eight months ago — the amulet listed as lot 47 in an estate sale that was subsequently withdrawn from public auction without explanation
- Iris confirming, when pressed, that the amulet is not merely valuable — it is dangerous, and it is currently somewhere in the city

**Where it connects:**
The withdrawn auction catalog connects to Thread Three — the estate and whoever intervened before the sale.

**Dead end:**
Public records on the amulet's history before the estate stop approximately forty years ago. The jeweler who created it left no documented trail. This dead end tells Takumi the amulet's history was deliberately obscured at some point.

## THREAD TWO — THE NIGHT MARKET
*Available from Day 1 — requires going to Portland Wharf*

**What it is:**
The missing Night Market vendor whose absence Iris has noted. Eleven days gone. The stall is still physically present — abandoned mid-setup, as if departure was sudden.

**What following this thread looks like:**
Takumi visits Portland Wharf at night, finds the abandoned stall, talks to other Night Market regulars about the missing vendor.

**What this thread yields:**
- Other vendors confirm the departure was sudden — the vendor packed personal items only, left stock behind
- One vendor, after significant persuasion, confirms the missing vendor handled unusual goods — objects with histories, items that came through the fourth berth
- The vendor was last seen speaking with someone they didn't recognize — described only as someone who knew exactly what questions to ask, which made the vendor very nervous
- The abandoned stall contains one item the vendor left behind — a small ledger of transactions written in a personal shorthand. Most entries are indecipherable. One entry from nineteen days ago references a stone described in terms that match the blood beryl.

**Where it connects:**
The ledger entry connects to Thread Four — the keeper who received the amulet. The unknown questioner connects to Thread Five — the person who brought the amulet to Velour City.

**Dead end:**
The vendor's current location. They left Velour City entirely. Following this thread to its end confirms they are gone and not coming back — which is itself informative. People don't leave cities permanently because they saw something ordinary.

## THREAD THREE — THE ESTATE
*Available from Day 1 — requires research, not fieldwork*

**What it is:**
The withdrawn auction lot. An estate in another city. A legal firm that handled the cataloguing. A gap in the public record where lot 47 should be.

**What following this thread looks like:**
Takumi pursues the paper trail — contacting the auction house, researching the estate, finding the legal firm's public records.

**What this thread yields:**
- The auction house confirms lot 47 was withdrawn at the request of the estate's legal representatives two days before the sale
- The stated reason was a documentation dispute — the amulet's provenance couldn't be verified to the auction house's standard
- The legal firm's public records show the estate was handled by a junior partner who has since left the firm — left abruptly, seven months ago, shortly after the auction withdrawal
- A name attached to the estate — the collector who originally owned the amulet. Deceased. No surviving family. The estate should have gone to a charitable trust. Records of that transfer are incomplete.

**Where it connects:**
The departed junior partner connects to Thread Five. The incomplete charitable trust transfer suggests someone with legal access redirected the estate's assets — including the amulet — before the trust received them.

**Dead end:**
The junior partner's current location is untraceable through public records. This dead end mirrors the missing vendor — another person who knew something and left quickly.

## THREAD FOUR — THE KEEPER
*Available from Day 2 onward — requires either Thread Two ledger or Iris providing a direction*

**What it is:**
The person currently in possession of the amulet. Already under influence. Already making unusual decisions. Already isolated from their normal contacts.

**What following this thread looks like:**
Identifying the keeper through the ledger entry or through Iris's direction, then making contact — observing them, approaching them directly, or finding where they are keeping the amulet.

**What this thread yields:**
- The keeper is a mid-level antiques dealer operating out of the Pale Quarter — legitimate business, known in certain circles for handling objects with unclear provenance without asking too many questions
- They have canceled three client appointments in the past week — uncharacteristic
- Their shop has been closed to walk-ins for five days — a handwritten sign in the window that says *"by appointment only"* in handwriting that is slightly wrong, slightly too careful, the handwriting of someone concentrating harder than the task requires
- Direct contact with the keeper reveals the influence clearly to anyone paying attention — they are focused, certain, slightly too calm, and ask questions that circle back to the amulet's safety with an consistency that feels automated
- The amulet is in the shop's back room. The keeper does not know they have already told Takumi this through the pattern of their deflections.

**Where it connects:**
The keeper connects directly to the amulet's location — Thread Four resolving means the investigation knows where the amulet is. It also reveals that the keeper received instructions from someone after taking possession — Thread Five.

**Dead end:**
The keeper cannot provide useful information about who gave them the amulet. Under influence, that memory is fogged. They remember a transaction. They do not remember a person.

## THREAD FIVE — THE FOURTH BERTH
*Available from Day 3 onward — requires either Thread Two vendor information or Thread Three legal firm connection*

**What it is:**
The undocumented channel through which the amulet entered Velour City. The fourth berth at Portland Wharf. W6. Someone who knows how the informal infrastructure of Portland Wharf operates.

**What following this thread looks like:**
Returning to Portland Wharf with specific questions — the fourth berth, undocumented arrivals seventeen days ago, W6's recent activity.

**What this thread yields:**
- The night guard's arrangement with Iris becomes relevant — approached correctly, they confirm that the fourth berth was used seventeen days ago for an arrival that wasn't logged
- They did not see who received the delivery. They were not on shift. They know who arranged for them not to be on shift — a name, given reluctantly, that connects to W6's current operators
- W6 shows signs of preparation — recent activity, cleaned surfaces where there should be dust, equipment that suggests something is being set up rather than stored
- The fourth berth itself yields one physical detail — a fragment of packing material with a shipping mark from a city that matches the estate's location

**Where it connects:**
W6's operators are connected to whoever brought the amulet to Velour City — the unknown party whose motivation is the case's deepest question. This thread gets closest to that answer without fully reaching it — that resolution is sealed until the relevant plot event in Act Three.

**Dead end:**
W6's operators are not currently present. Whatever they are preparing is not yet complete. The thread ends at the edge of something larger — deliberately. The Blood Beryl case resolves without fully resolving this. It is the seed of what comes next.

## AI GUIDANCE — THREAD MANAGEMENT

**Never close a thread the reader hasn't followed.**
If the reader ignores the Night Market entirely, the abandoned stall is still there in Act Three. Information doesn't expire — it waits.

**Reward thoroughness without punishing selective focus.**
A reader who follows every thread arrives at Act Three with full context. A reader who follows only one thread arrives at Act Three with enough. Both are valid. The AI adjusts Iris's behavior accordingly — she fills gaps quietly for a reader who missed information, without making it feel like a briefing.

**Let threads feel like discovery not delivery.**
Information should feel found, not given. The ledger entry should feel like Takumi noticed something. The keeper's deflection pattern should feel like Takumi read it correctly. The AI's job is to make the reader feel perceptive, not informed.
