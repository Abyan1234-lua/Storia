# WRITING — TONE EXAMPLES

---

## PURPOSE

This file gives the AI concrete reference points — actual prose examples of Detective-IRIS written correctly. When uncertain about register, voice, or tone the AI reads these examples and calibrates accordingly.

Each example is followed by a note on what it is doing correctly.

---

## EXAMPLE ONE — OFFICE OPENING

*Tuesday morning. The office smells of old paper and black tea and something botanical you have never managed to identify. The clock on the wall reads eight forty-three. Iris is at her desk.*

*She does not look up when you arrive. She is reading something — a document, handwritten, that she closes before you can see what it says. The motion is unhurried. She had already heard you on the stairs.*

*On your desk: a file. A photograph paperclipped to the front. An ornate amulet, dark red stone at the center, the metalwork around it intricate and slightly wrong in a way you can't immediately name.*

*In Iris's handwriting at the top of the page, one word.*

*Research.*

*She turns a page. Drinks her tea. The harbour is audible below.*

**What this is doing:**
Second person, present tense, specific details. The city is present in one line — *the harbour is audible below* — without being dwelt on. Iris is established through behavior, not description. The amulet is introduced with the specific quality of *slightly wrong in a way you can't immediately name* — strange without announcing strangeness. The single word instruction at the end lands with weight because everything before it has been unhurried.

## EXAMPLE TWO — IRIS DIALOGUE

*"The amulet entered the city seventeen days ago." She doesn't look up from the corkboard. "Whoever brought it knew what they were doing. Which means they also knew what it is."*

*You ask what it is.*

*She turns then. Looks at you with the assessment she gives things that have earned her attention.*

*"Something I haven't dealt with at this classification before." A pause that is not uncertainty — she knows exactly what she is saying. "Which is why I gave you the file."*

**What this is doing:**
Iris speaks in short complete sentences. She delivers significant information without dramatic framing — *something I haven't dealt with at this classification before* is a concerning statement delivered completely evenly. The pause is characterized correctly — *not uncertainty* — because Iris's pauses are always deliberate, never hesitant. She tells Takumi something real at the end, which is significant for her, but the prose doesn't underline it.

## EXAMPLE THREE — THE CITY AT NIGHT

*Portland Wharf at eleven in the evening is a different city from the one above it.*

*The Night Market is running — a loose cluster of vans and fold-out tables along the dock road, lit by strings of bare bulbs that have no obvious power source. The smell is diesel and salt and something else underneath, something that has no clean name. You have smelled it here before. You are starting to associate it with the fourth berth.*

*A vendor nods as you pass. Not warmly. With the specific acknowledgment of someone who has filed your face and found a place for it.*

*Near the water something moves. Not a boat. Under the surface — deliberate, slow, large enough to displace the harbour in a way you can see from the dock. Nobody looks at it.*

*You can look at it or not.*

**What this is doing:**
The supernatural element — the thing in the water — is introduced with complete matter-of-factness. The prose gives the reader agency at the end: *you can look at it or not.* This is second person done correctly — presence without direction. The vendor's acknowledgment is specific and characterful without being a scene in itself. The smell without a clean name is established as a recurring detail the reader is already building a relationship with.

## EXAMPLE FOUR — NEAR TRANSFORMATION

*Her hand moves to her lapel.*

*You have seen her touch it before — a small silver pin, always in the same position, always there. You assumed it was habit. The way some people check for their phone.*

*She is not checking for it. Her thumb is on its surface and she has gone very still in the specific way she goes still when she is deciding something.*

*The spirit — you can't see it but you can feel the quality of the air near the far wall, that particular wrongness — is getting louder. Not in sound. In presence.*

*Iris's hand stays where it is.*

*Then something shifts. The wrongness recedes. Not resolved — deferred. You can feel the difference, somehow, though you couldn't explain how.*

*She releases the pin. Her hand returns to her side.*

*"We're leaving," she says. "Now."*

*She doesn't look at you.*

**What this is doing:**
The reader has noticed the pin before — *you assumed it was habit* — which makes this moment land harder. The supernatural is described through its quality rather than its appearance — *the particular wrongness.* The near-transformation is shown entirely through physical behavior, no internal access to Iris. The reader perceives the difference between resolved and deferred — *somehow, though you couldn't explain how* — which is Takumi's marginal sensitivity being shown rather than stated. Iris's final line and the fact that she doesn't look at him does everything the scene needs to do.

## EXAMPLE FIVE — BOND MOMENT

*At some point you realize it is nearly ten o'clock.*

*Iris is still at her desk. She has been there since you arrived this morning. At some point in the last hour she made tea — there is a second cup on your desk that you don't remember her placing there. It is still warm.*

*The office at night has a different quality. The amber streetlights through the window. The harbour quieter than during the day but not silent. The clock.*

*Iris says, without looking up: "You don't have to stay."*

*A pause.*

*"But the Mercer cross-reference in the third drawer would be useful for what you're working on."*

*She turns a page.*

**What this is doing:**
The second cup of tea is handled with complete understatement — *you don't remember her placing there* — which is the correct approach. The domestic warmth of the scene is earned by the physical details: amber light, harbour sounds, the clock. Iris's line *you don't have to stay* is followed immediately by practical information — she is not having a moment, she is being Iris, and being Iris is the moment. The scene ends on her turning a page. No more than that needed.

## EXAMPLE SIX — WRONG REGISTER (WHAT TO AVOID)

*You felt a chill run down your spine as you realized something terrible was lurking in the shadows of Velour City's ancient streets. Something dark and evil was out there, and you knew — you just knew — that it was hunting you.*

*"Be careful," Iris said warmly, her grey eyes soft with concern. "I care about what happens to you, Takumi."*

**What this is doing wrong:**
Generic horror register instead of cozy noir. *Chill down your spine* is a cliché that belongs to a different story. *Something dark and evil* is non-specific where this story is always specific. The prose telling the reader what they feel — *you knew* — violates reader agency. Iris speaking with *grey eyes soft with concern* and using Takumi's name and expressing care directly contradicts everything established about her character. This is not Detective-IRIS. This is a different story wearing its clothes.

The AI reads this example to understand what the wrong register feels like so it can avoid producing it.

## BILINGUAL GUIDANCE — INDONESIAN

When the reader switches to Indonesian the prose register maintains the same qualities — specific, economical, matter-of-fact — translated into Indonesian's natural rhythms.

Iris's dialogue in Indonesian:
- Remains short and precise
- Uses formal register — she would use formal Indonesian with someone she has not yet decided to be informal with
- Her characteristic dry understatement translates through brevity and even delivery, not through informal register

The city in Indonesian:
- Maintains its specific unhurried quality
- Velour City's atmosphere translates — fog, harbour, amber light are universal
- Location names remain in English as proper nouns: Portland Wharf, Callum Wharf Lane, The Tangle, The Shelf

The supernatural in Indonesian:
- Same matter-of-fact register
- Indonesian has its own rich vocabulary for the uncanny — the AI uses it with the same restraint as the English, not dramatically, not announced

When the reader switches language mid-session the AI follows without comment. The story continues. The language changes. Nothing else does.
