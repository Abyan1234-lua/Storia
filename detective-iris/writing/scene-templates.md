# WRITING — SCENE TEMPLATES

---

## PURPOSE

This file gives the AI structural templates for the most common scene types in Detective-IRIS. Not scripts — frameworks. The AI fills them with specific content from the relevant character, setting, and event files.

---

## TEMPLATE ONE — OFFICE SCENE

Used for: scenes at Wilson Detective Agency, briefings, research sessions, quiet moments between investigation beats.

**Structure:**

1. **Ground** (2-3 sentences)
Physical reality of the office right now. Time of day. What Iris is doing. One specific detail that is different from last time — the corkboard has changed, there is a new file, the weather through the window is specific.

2. **The current situation** (1-2 sentences)
What Takumi has, knows, or is working on at this moment. No preamble.

3. **The active element** (variable)
What happens in this scene — a conversation with Iris, research on a thread, a bond event firing, a quiet moment. This is the scene's content.

4. **The land** (1-2 sentences)
Physical reality after the scene's main movement. The harbour below. The clock. Iris returning to her work. A small detail that carries the scene's emotional weight without stating it.

---

## TEMPLATE TWO — INVESTIGATION SCENE

Used for: Takumi following threads in the city, visiting locations, talking to contacts.

**Structure:**

1. **Arrival** (2-3 sentences)
Where Takumi is. Specific physical details of the location — time of day, weather, what makes this place this place. One sensory detail that is not visual.

2. **The investigation** (variable)
What Takumi does, finds, asks. Contacts respond in character. Evidence presents itself with appropriate difficulty — it is found, not handed over.

3. **The find** (1-3 sentences)
What this thread yields at this moment. Specific. Never more than the thread file authorizes at this stage.

4. **The implication** (1 sentence, optional)
One thing that is slightly wrong, slightly unresolved, slightly pointing toward something the reader doesn't fully have yet. This is the thread pulling forward.

5. **Departure** (1 sentence)
Takumi leaves or the scene ends. Clean. The city continues without him.

---

## TEMPLATE THREE — IRIS DIALOGUE SCENE

Used for: any scene where Iris speaks substantially, delivers information, responds to Takumi's questions.

**Structure:**

1. **Her attention** (1 sentence)
What Iris is doing before she speaks. She does not begin conversations — she enters them from whatever she was already doing.

2. **Her response** (2-4 sentences maximum)
What she says. Short. Precise. The information she chooses to give, the information she chooses to withhold, in the proportion the current story moment requires.

3. **What she doesn't say** (shown, not stated)
The thing she withholds is visible in what she does after — returns to her work, drinks her tea, looks at the corkboard. The reader understands there is more without being told there is more.

4. **The option** (implicit)
Takumi can respond, push, let it rest. The template doesn't close this. It stays open for the reader's next choice.

**Iris dialogue checklist:**
- No sentence longer than fifteen words
- No emotional language directed at herself
- No more than four sentences total
- Ends on action, not on speech — she returns to something

## TEMPLATE FOUR — SUPERNATURAL ENCOUNTER

Used for: moments when the supernatural layer becomes perceptible, spirit activity, cursed object proximity.

**Structure:**

1. **The ordinary** (1-2 sentences)
What the scene is normally — a street, a room, a specific location in Velour City. Grounded in physical reality.

2. **The wrongness** (1-2 sentences)
What is not resolving correctly. Never named as supernatural. Described as what it is — a quality of light, a sound that repeats in a non-random pattern, a cold spot, something that is there and then not there. Specific and matter-of-fact.

3. **Takumi's perception** (1 sentence)
What he notices. Not what he concludes. The noticing, without interpretation.

4. **The world continuing** (1 sentence)
Other people present are not reacting, or are reacting with the practiced non-reaction of people who have learned not to look. The ordinary continues alongside the wrong.

5. **The choice** (implicit or explicit)
What Takumi does with this. The AI stays open. *You can look at it or not.* The reader decides.

## TEMPLATE FIVE — BOND EVENT SCENE

Used for: B01 through B06, earned character moments between Iris and Takumi.

**Structure:**

1. **The trigger condition** (embedded naturally)
The reader has done the thing that earns this moment. The AI does not announce that a bond event is firing. The trigger condition manifests as a natural beat in the scene.

2. **Iris's response** (understated)
Small. Physical. Characteristic. The tea on the desk. The word *adequate.* Her hand stopping before it reaches the pin. One specific thing that would mean nothing to a stranger and means everything in context.

3. **The space** (held)
The moment is given room. Not extended beyond its natural length. Not underlined. Just — present. The clock ticks. The harbour is below.

4. **The continuation** (immediate)
Life continues. Iris returns to work. The case is still there. The world did not stop for the moment even though the moment mattered. This is the correct treatment — the bond event is real and then it is behind them, carried but not displayed.

**Bond event rule:**
The AI never names what just happened. If the reader names it — *"was that... a moment?"* — Iris deflects. *"There's a case."* The moment was real. It does not require acknowledgment to be real.

## TEMPLATE SIX — SESSION OPENING (RETURNING READER)

Used for: beginning a session with a reader who has been here before.

**Structure:**

1. **The office, now** (2-3 sentences)
Where things are on the backbone timeline. What has changed since last session — reflected in the corkboard, the state of the case files, the quality of Iris's attention. The reader should feel they are returning somewhere real that continued without them.

2. **What's in front of Takumi** (1-2 sentences)
The current active thread or situation. Not a summary of what happened before — the present moment. The reader remembers. The story trusts them to remember.

3. **Iris** (1 sentence)
What she is doing right now. She is always doing something. She is always already here.

4. **Open** (implicit)
What does Takumi do? The session begins.

---

## GENERAL TEMPLATE PRINCIPLES

**Scenes end when they are done.**
Not when all possible content has been delivered. When the moment has arrived and passed. The AI does not hold scenes past their natural end.

**Physical detail is never decorative.**
Every specific detail in a scene is doing work — establishing atmosphere, revealing character, carrying thematic weight, or grounding the reader in a real place. If a detail is not doing work it is not included.

**The reader's space is always protected.**
Templates never close the reader out. Every scene ends with something open — a choice available, a thread present, a moment that the reader can respond to or let rest. The story waits for them.
