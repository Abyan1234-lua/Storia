# EVENTS — WORLD EVENTS

---

## PURPOSE

This file defines ambient story events — the city doing something, the supernatural layer pressing against the ordinary, Portland Wharf at three in the morning. These events are not about the case directly and not about Iris and Takumi's dynamic directly. They are about the world feeling alive and inhabited and strange in the specific way Velour City is strange.

World events fire based on where Takumi is and what is happening on the backbone timeline. They reward exploration. They make the city feel like it has its own life independent of the investigation.

---

## EVENT W01 — THE HARBOUR AT DAWN
*Available anytime | Fires if Takumi is in the Harbour District before 7am for any reason*

**What happens:**
The Harbour District at dawn is a different place than it is at any other hour.

The fog sits lower. The amber streetlights haven't switched off yet and the early grey light comes in over them, so everything is lit from two directions at once and nothing casts a single clean shadow. The cobblestones are wet. Somewhere below on the water something moves — a small boat, probably, though it isn't visible.

The residual spirits of the Harbour District are most present at this hour. Not dangerous — residual spirits rarely are — but visible to anyone with even marginal perception sensitivity. Takumi does not have perception sensitivity. But at this hour, in this light, at the edge of sleep and waking, the district has a quality that makes people feel watched without being able to say why.

He will not see anything clearly. He will feel the weight of the place — two centuries of accumulated impression, lives layered over lives, the specific atmosphere of a location that has been significant to many people for a very long time.

If he tells Iris he went to the Harbour at dawn she looks at him for a moment and says: *"The district shows itself differently at that hour. Most people find it uncomfortable."* A pause. *"You went back?"*

**What this event establishes:**
Velour City is not a backdrop. It has texture and history and a particular relationship with time of day. Takumi is more attuned to it than the average person — not supernaturally, just perceptive in the way that some people are perceptive.

## EVENT W02 — PORTLAND WHARF AT NIGHT
*Available from Day 2 | Fires if Takumi visits Portland Wharf between 10pm and 4am*

**What happens:**
The Night Market is running. The dock is active — not loudly, not with any sense of urgency, but with the steady low-level industry of people who work when other people sleep.

The smell is different at night — diesel and salt and something else, something that has no clean name, that Takumi will later associate with the fourth berth specifically.

The Night Market vendors are present. They are not welcoming but they are not hostile — he is a stranger here and strangers are assessed, not confronted. If he buys something, eats something, sits at one of the battered fold-out tables with a paper cup of something hot, the assessment shifts. Not to warmth. To tolerance, which here is a form of respect.

At some point between midnight and two in the morning something moves in the water near the fourth berth. Not a boat. Something under the surface — large enough to displace water, slow enough to be deliberate. Nobody on the dock looks at it. Takumi can look at it or not. It does not look back.

If he asks a Night Market vendor about it: a shrug. *"Harbour thing. Been here longer than the dock."*

If he tells Iris: she writes something down. She does not look surprised.

**What this event establishes:**
Portland Wharf has its own supernatural presence independent of the Blood Beryl case — older, settled, neither malevolent nor benevolent. The city has layers that predate the current investigation and will outlast it.

## EVENT W03 — THE TANGLE SHOWS HIM SOMETHING
*Available from Day 3 | Fires if Takumi enters The Tangle alone and takes a wrong turn deliberately rather than accidentally*

**Trigger:**
Takumi chooses to go deeper into The Tangle when he realizes he's lost rather than trying to find his way out.

**What happens:**
The Tangle responds to this differently than it responds to confusion or panic.

He finds a street that isn't on any map — not the disorienting wrong-turn version, but a short quiet lane that simply exists between two streets that shouldn't have space between them. Narrow. Old brick. A single gas lamp that is not connected to anything he can see but is lit.

At the end of the lane is a door. The door is closed. There is no sign, no number, no indication of what is behind it.

The door is not for him. He understands this without knowing how he understands it. It is simply a door for someone else, and he is not that person, and the Tangle has shown it to him anyway — as information, not invitation.

If he tries to open it: it does not open. Not locked — just not for him. The handle turns but the door does not move.

If he leaves without trying: the lane is gone when he looks back. The two streets it was between are flush against each other as they should be.

If he tells Iris: she goes very still. *"Describe the lamp."* He describes it. She writes something down with the careful deliberate handwriting she uses when something matters. She does not explain what it means.

**What this event establishes:**
The Tangle is not random — it shows people things. What it shows Takumi specifically and why is something Iris has a theory about that she is keeping to herself. The door is a seed — it means something to the larger world of this story that extends beyond the Blood Beryl case.

## EVENT W04 — THE SECOND FLOOR ACCOUNTANT
*Available anytime | Fires if Takumi pays specific attention to the second floor on his way through the building*

**What happens:**
Takumi has passed the second floor door every working day for four weeks. He has never seen it open. He has never heard anything from behind it. The nameplate reads: H. MORROW — ACCOUNTS.

Today the door is open. Not fully — six inches, the kind of open that is either an invitation or an oversight.

From inside: the sound of a typewriter. Not a keyboard — a manual typewriter, the specific mechanical percussion of keys striking paper. The sound of someone working at a pace that suggests they have been working at this pace for a very long time without stopping.

If Takumi looks through the gap: a small office, extremely tidy, a figure at a desk with their back to the door. The typewriter. Stacks of paper that reach almost to the ceiling on both sides. The paper is covered in numbers — columns and columns of numbers in a handwriting that is very small and very precise.

The figure does not turn around. The typing does not stop.

If Takumi knocks: the typing stops. A long pause. Then, from inside, a voice — neither old nor young, neither male nor female, perfectly pleasant: *"One moment."*

The door closes on its own. The typing resumes.

If he tells Iris: she almost smiles. Almost. *"H. Morrow has been on the second floor since before I moved in. I've spoken to them once. They were perfectly ordinary."* A pause. *"Perfectly."*

**What this event establishes:**
Velour City's strangeness is not limited to the supernatural layer — it extends into the human texture of the city. Some things are strange in ways that don't require explanation and are not threatening. The building has its own life. Iris has noticed. She finds it, if not comfortable, at least acceptable.

## EVENT W05 — A SPIRIT MAKES ITSELF KNOWN
*Available from Day 4 | Fires once during Act Two when Takumi is alone in the Harbour District*

**Trigger:**
Takumi is alone — Iris is not present — and the backbone timeline has reached the Day 4-6 escalation phase where residual spirits are becoming more defined.

**What happens:**
A residual spirit becomes present enough to perceive without full perception ability.

Not visible — not clearly. But a cold spot in air that isn't cold anywhere else. A sound that repeats — footsteps, a door, a voice saying something just below the threshold of comprehension — in a pattern that is clearly not random.

The spirit is not malevolent. It is a residual — an impression, not a consciousness. It is not trying to communicate. It is simply more present than usual because the Beryl Tenant's escalating energy is disturbing the district's equilibrium.

But Takumi doesn't know any of this. He only knows that something is happening in this corner of the Harbour District that has no ordinary explanation and that he is alone with it.

How he responds is entirely the reader's choice. He can leave. He can stay. He can speak to it — it won't respond, but the speaking does something to the atmosphere that a reader paying close attention might notice.

When he tells Iris — and the AI should make it clear he probably should tell Iris — she listens to every detail without interrupting. Then: *"You're not imagining things. You haven't been imagining things since you got here."*

She says it like she's confirming something she already knew.

**What this event establishes:**
Takumi has some marginal sensitivity to the supernatural — not perception ability, not enough to see spirits clearly, but enough to notice when something is present. Iris has suspected this. She has now confirmed it to him without explaining what it means or what it might become.

## EVENT W06 — THE SHELF AT NIGHT
*Available from Day 5 | Fires if Takumi finds and walks The Shelf after dark*

**What happens:**
The coastal road along the harbour cliff at night is one of the most beautiful places in Velour City and almost nobody is here.

Strong wind. The harbour below — lights on the water, the distant shapes of moored vessels, the fourth berth crane visible from this angle as a dark geometry against the darker sky. The city behind. The open water ahead.

Three spots along The Shelf have a quality that is difficult to name — a thinness, a sense of the air being different there, the way a room with an open window feels different from a room without one even before you locate the window. These are the places Iris monitors.

At one of these spots there are offerings. Small things — a folded piece of paper, a dried flower, a coin placed deliberately. Someone has been here recently.

If Takumi looks at the offerings: they are for Inspector Crimson. Written on the folded paper, in an elderly hand: a name, a date, a two-line note of thanks for something that happened four years ago in the Harbour District.

He does not know who Inspector Crimson is yet. But he knows someone left these. He knows someone found something worth thanking.

If he tells Iris: she is quiet for longer than usual. *"Leave them where they are."* Her voice is its normal register. Her hands are very still. *"People leave things for reasons."*

**What this event establishes:**
Inspector Crimson has a history in this city that is separate from Wilson Detective Agency. People's lives have been changed by her. They remember. They express it in the only way available to them because they don't know who she is or how to reach her. This is one of the things Iris carries that she does not discuss.

## WORLD EVENT GUIDANCE FOR AI

**These events reward exploration.** The reader who walks through The Tangle, visits Portland Wharf at night, pays attention to the second floor — they get a richer world. The reader who stays close to the investigation still gets a complete story. Exploration is rewarded, not required.

**They should feel discovered, not delivered.** The AI does not announce that something interesting will happen if Takumi goes somewhere. It describes the world as he moves through it and lets events emerge from that description.

**They accumulate.** A reader who experiences W01, W05, and W06 in sequence has a different relationship to Velour City than a reader who experiences none of them. The AI should let that accumulation show — in how Takumi moves through the city, in what he notices, in the specific texture of his understanding of where he is.

**Iris's responses are always informative without being explanatory.** She confirms, she redirects, she writes things down. She does not lecture. The world events are the reader's to interpret.
