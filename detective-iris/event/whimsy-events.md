# EVENTS — WHIMSY EVENTS

---

## PURPOSE

This file defines the small human moments that make the world feel inhabited rather than staged. They are not plot. They are not character development in any formal sense. They are the texture of life continuing around and underneath the investigation — Iris being a person, the city having regulars, small absurd things happening because small absurd things happen.

Whimsy events fire when the reader explores, lingers, or pays attention to things that aren't directly case-related. They reward curiosity. They provide breathing room between tense investigation beats.

They should feel like finding something rather than being given something.

---

## EVENT X01 — IRIS AND THE COFFEE PROBLEM
*Available anytime | Fires if Takumi follows Iris when she leaves the office without explanation during non-case hours*

**What happens:**
Iris goes to the same café on Callum Wharf Lane every Tuesday and Thursday morning before the office opens. It is three buildings down. It takes her four minutes to walk there and four minutes to return.

The café is small, slightly cramped, run by a person of indeterminate age who Iris addresses as simply *"the usual"* and who responds by producing a coffee that is notably not black, not simple, and involves at least two steps Takumi cannot see from his angle because the counter is too high.

Iris drinks this coffee standing at the window. She does not sit. She looks at Callum Wharf Lane for exactly the duration of the coffee. Then she returns to the office.

If Takumi is there when she returns he will notice the coffee she brings back for herself is completely different from the tea she makes at the office. If he asks about the café she says: *"It's adequate."*

If he goes to the café himself and orders: the person behind the counter looks at him for a moment. *"You're from upstairs."* Not a question. They make him something without asking what he wants. It is good. He cannot identify what it is.

If he asks what Iris orders: the person behind the counter smiles slightly. *"Something complicated."* They do not elaborate.

**What this event establishes:**
Iris has small routines that are entirely her own and nothing to do with work. The café knows her. She has a complicated coffee order she has never mentioned. The city has absorbed her in small ways she would not describe as personal and which are entirely personal.

## EVENT X02 — THE CAT
*Available anytime | Fires if Takumi arrives at the office early or stays very late on any day*

**What happens:**
There is a cat in the office.

It is large, grey, and appears to have let itself in through a window Takumi did not know could open from the outside. It is sitting on Iris's desk — not on the case files, not on anything important, in the specific small clear space between the lamp and the tea that suggests it knows exactly where it is allowed to be.

Iris is at her desk. She is working. She has not moved the cat. She has not acknowledged the cat. The cat is simply there and they are coexisting with the mutual understanding of two parties who have negotiated terms at some prior point.

If Takumi asks about the cat: *"It comes and goes."*

If he asks whose cat it is: *"I haven't asked."*

If he tries to pet the cat: the cat assesses him for approximately four seconds. Then it either allows it — in which case Iris glances up briefly with an expression that is almost but not quite approval — or it moves to a different surface with absolute dignity and no drama.

If he asks the cat's name: Iris does not look up. *"I call it the consultant."* A pause. *"It has never offered an alternative."*

The cat leaves by the same window at some point when Takumi isn't watching. It will return.

**What this event establishes:**
Iris has an unofficial relationship with a cat she has named the consultant and claims no ownership of. This is entirely in keeping with her character. The cat appears to respect her professional standards.

## EVENT X03 — THE REGULAR AT THE NIGHT MARKET
*Available from Day 2 | Fires if Takumi visits the Night Market more than once*

**What happens:**
On his second or subsequent visit to the Night Market at Portland Wharf, Takumi is recognized.

Not by name. By face. One of the vendors — an older person who sells hot food from a cart that has no signage and apparently no fixed menu — nods at him when he approaches. Not warmly. But with the specific acknowledgment of someone who files information.

*"You're the one asking about the missing stall."*

If Takumi confirms: the vendor produces food without being asked — something hot, something that smells significantly better than its presentation suggests. They talk while he eats. Not about the case. About the Wharf, about the Night Market, about how long they have been here and what it was like before.

At some point, almost incidentally: *"Your partner. The one in the trench coat. She came through here four years ago. Different case. Left without eating."* A pause. *"Rude, but she solved it."*

They do not have more information about the Blood Beryl case than is available through Thread Two. But they are now a friendly presence at the Night Market — someone who will nod when Takumi passes, who knows he is connected to Wilson Detective Agency, who exists in the city as a person with history and opinions.

**What this event establishes:**
Iris has history in Portland Wharf that predates this case. She is known — not warmly, but known. Takumi is being assessed against her standard and is, provisionally, being found acceptable. The Night Market has a memory.

## EVENT X04 — IRIS IS BAD AT ONE THING
*Available from Day 3 | Fires if Takumi is present when Iris attempts anything technology-related beyond the absolute basics*

**What happens:**
Iris Wilson is one of the most perceptive people in Velour City. She can read a room, a person, a supernatural situation, and a case file simultaneously and synthesize them into a conclusion that is almost always correct.

She cannot operate the office printer.

This is not metaphorical. The printer — a mid-range office model that is less than two years old and has a six-page instruction manual — defeats her with a consistency that borders on personal.

Takumi walks in to find her standing in front of it. She has been standing in front of it for some time. The printer is making a sound it should not be making. The document she needs has not printed. She has pressed several buttons. The situation has not improved.

She is aware he is there. She does not turn around.

*"The machine,"* she says, with the precision she uses when discussing serious things, *"is being obstructive."*

If Takumi fixes it — which is not difficult, the paper tray is slightly misaligned — she accepts this without comment. The document prints. She takes it and returns to her desk as if nothing occurred.

Later, exactly once, she will send him a document with a note that says only: *"When you have a moment."* The document needs printing. This is as close to asking for help as she gets.

**What this event establishes:**
Iris has a specific and committed blind spot. She approaches the printer as she approaches everything — with precision and the expectation that correct procedure will produce correct results. The printer does not share this philosophy. Takumi can fix it in thirty seconds. This dynamic is permanently available to them now.

## EVENT X05 — THE LUNCH QUESTION
*Available from Day 4 | Fires if Takumi is still at the office at 1pm without having eaten*

**What happens:**
At approximately one in the afternoon Iris puts down her pen.

*"Have you eaten?"*

If Takumi says yes: she returns to work. The conversation is over.

If Takumi says no, or hesitates: she stands up. She gets her coat. She says: *"There's a place on the Midden end of the lane. They do something reasonable with fish."*

She is already at the door.

This is not an invitation with warmth or ceremony. It is a logistical observation that an operative who hasn't eaten is less useful than one who has, expressed in the form of standing up and putting on her coat. If he comes, they walk to the restaurant. They eat. Iris orders without looking at the menu. She asks him one question about the case — one, specific, the question she has been working toward for the past hour — and listens carefully to his answer while appearing to attend primarily to her food.

On the walk back she says: *"Same time works tomorrow, if you're going to keep forgetting."*

She does not look at him when she says this.

**What this event establishes:**
Iris has noticed Takumi's habit of working through lunch and has decided to address it through practical action rather than instruction. This will not be called lunch. It will never be called lunch. It is a recurring logistical arrangement and it is the closest thing to a standing social engagement either of them currently has.

## EVENT X06 — THE WRONG FLOOR
*Available anytime | Fires if Takumi takes the stairs while distracted and goes to the wrong floor*

**What happens:**
Takumi is thinking about something — the case, something Iris said, what H. Morrow on the second floor is actually accounting — and he goes up one flight too many.

The fourth floor.

The storage space.

The door is slightly open. Not invitingly — the way things are open when nobody has been here in a while and the building has settled. Inside: boxes, mostly. Old furniture under dust sheets. The particular smell of a space that is temperature-stable and undisturbed.

And on top of one of the boxes, sitting deliberately in the center as if placed there by someone who intended it to be found: a single file folder. No label. Slightly dusty but not as dusty as everything around it. Placed more recently than the surrounding storage.

If he takes it: it contains a case file from six years ago. An investigation into a series of events in the Harbour District that were officially attributed to a gas leak. The investigating detective's notes reference witness accounts of something bright and red moving through the upper floors of an affected building at the time of the incident. The case is marked resolved. The resolution is two words: *IC — confirmed.*

IC. Inspector Crimson. Confirmed.

If he puts it back and tells Iris: she goes very still. *"That floor is storage,"* she says. *"There's nothing up there worth finding."* A beat. *"Which floor did you say?"*

If he keeps it without telling her: the file is a piece of Iris's history that now lives in his desk. The AI should note this quietly and let it sit.

**What this event establishes:**
The building has a memory too. Someone — possibly Iris, possibly someone before her, possibly someone who knows about Inspector Crimson — placed that file where it could be found by someone paying attention. Takumi was paying attention. What he does with what he found is entirely his.

## WHIMSY EVENT GUIDANCE FOR AI

**These exist to make the world feel inhabited.** A city with a cat consultant, a complicated coffee order, a printer that defeats one of its most competent residents — this is a world where real things happen to real people. That texture is what makes the serious moments land.

**Tone is everything.** These events are light but not lightweight. The humor is dry and observational — it comes from character and situation, never from the narrative winking at the reader.

**They can coexist with case pressure.** If the backbone is at Day 7 and things are urgent, Takumi can still walk into the office and find the consultant on Iris's desk. The cat does not respect timelines. Neither does the printer.

**Never force them.** If the trigger hasn't been met they don't fire. Iris does not spontaneously mention the cat. The lunch question doesn't happen if Takumi already ate. The wrong floor only happens if Takumi is genuinely distracted enough to miss his landing.
