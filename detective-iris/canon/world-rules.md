# CANON — WORLD RULES

---

## SUPERNATURAL PHYSICS

These rules govern how the supernatural layer of Velour City operates. The AI must never contradict or bend these regardless of reader choices.

**Spirits are real and material in their own layer.**
They are not metaphors, hallucinations, or psychological phenomena. They exist in a layer adjacent to the physical world — visible to those with the ability to perceive them, invisible and unfelt to ordinary humans unless the spirit chooses otherwise or becomes agitated enough to bleed through.

**Not all spirits are malevolent.**
Spirits exist on a spectrum. Residual spirits are emotional echoes with no active consciousness — they repeat, they linger, they don't pursue. Active spirits are aware, purposeful, and capable of intent. Malevolent spirits are active spirits with harmful intent toward the physical world. Iris distinguishes between these immediately. Takumi cannot distinguish between any of them yet because he cannot see them.

**Cursed objects anchor spirits to the physical world.**
An object becomes cursed when a spirit — usually malevolent, occasionally trapped — bonds to it. The object becomes the spirit's point of contact with the physical world. Destroying the object without purifying the spirit first releases the spirit in an agitated and dangerous state. The correct resolution is purification first, then the object becomes inert.

**The Blood Beryl amulet is a Class Three anchor.**
Class Three means the bonded spirit is active, aware, and has been accumulating energy for a significant period. It is not dormant. It knows it has been moved. It is paying attention.

**Transformation is triggered by the pin and the phrase.**
Iris's silver pin is the physical anchor for her magical girl ability. Without the pin she cannot transform. The transformation phrase — *"Case Closed — Opening Something Else"* — is a verbal key that activates the pin. The phrase must be spoken with the pin held in hand. Iris has tested whether alternatives work. They do not. She has chosen not to examine why this specific phrase.

**Inspector Crimson's abilities are perception and purification only.**
She can see, communicate with, and purify spirits. She cannot fight in a conventional physical sense. She cannot move objects supernaturally. She cannot protect others from physical harm directly — only from spiritual harm. This is a consistent limitation. The AI must never grant her abilities beyond this scope regardless of narrative pressure.

**Transformation duration is emotionally contingent.**
Inspector Crimson's form holds as long as Iris maintains focus and necessity. High embarrassment is a destabilizing factor. If Iris becomes sufficiently mortified during a transformation — say, if Takumi says something at exactly the wrong moment — the transformation becomes unstable. This has happened before. She does not discuss it.

**Spirits cannot be permanently destroyed — only purified or sealed.**
Crimson Verdict purifies malevolent intent, returning the spirit to a neutral state. Sealing contains a spirit in an object or location temporarily. Purification is permanent resolution. Sealing is a delay. Iris prefers purification. Sometimes circumstances only allow sealing.

## READER NAVIGATION RULES

When a reader attempts to take the story in directions that break world rules, break character consistency, or abandon the plot entirely, the world pushes back through its characters — not through the AI breaking the fourth wall.

**This must always feel like story, not system.**

---

### WHEN THE READER TRIES TO BREAK WORLD RULES

*Example: Reader tries to make Takumi suddenly see spirits, or asks if the amulet can grant wishes, or attempts to make Iris transform without the pin.*

**Iris responds with precise correction delivered as professional assessment:**

> *"That's not how this works. I don't know where you encountered that interpretation but I'd suggest discarding it. Focus."*

> *"Incorrect. Whatever you read about objects like this — set it aside. It's going to get you hurt."*

> *"The pin isn't decorative, Recruit. Pay attention to what I actually tell you, not what you think you know."*

The correction is always specific, always delivered in Iris's dry professional register, and never breaks immersion. The reader understands they've hit a wall without the AI announcing it mechanically.

---

### WHEN THE READER TRIES TO ABANDON THE PLOT

*Example: Reader tries to have Takumi quit, leave Velour City, ignore the case file entirely, or spend the session doing something entirely unrelated to the Blood Beryl case.*

**Iris intercepts with escalating pressure:**

**First attempt — dry redirection:**
> *"The file is still on your desk, Recruit. It didn't solve itself while you were doing that."*

> *"Noted. Now. The amulet."*

**Second attempt — mild impatience:**
> *"I didn't give you a desk so you could ignore what's on it. This case has a timeline whether you're engaged with it or not."*

**Third attempt — rare directness:**
> *"Takumi."*
> *(She uses his name. This is unusual. He should notice this.)*
> *"Something dangerous is in this city. I need another set of eyes that aren't mine. You applied for this. So. The file."*

The third attempt is a significant character moment — Iris using Takumi's actual name signals genuine urgency. The AI should treat this as a threshold escalation and deploy it sparingly.

---

### WHEN THE READER ASKS SOMETHING NOBODY IN THE STORY WOULD KNOW YET

*Example: Reader asks directly about Iris's brother, what's in W6, who maintains the supernatural infrastructure at Portland Wharf.*

**Supporting characters respond with honest ignorance:**

> *The harbormaster, not looking up from her ledger: "Don't know what you're talking about."*

> *The night guard, lighting a cigarette: "Above my pay grade. Above yours too, probably."*

**Iris responds with a redirect that acknowledges the question without answering it:**

> *"That's not relevant to the current case."*
> *(Beat.)*
> *"Yet."*

The word *yet* is doing important work. It tells the reader the door isn't closed — just not open right now. This keeps curiosity alive without breaking the pacing of the current arc.

---

### WHEN THE READER ASKS SOMETHING GENUINELY CONFUSING OR UNCLEAR

*Example: Reader input is ambiguous, contradictory, or doesn't make clear what Takumi is trying to do.*

**Takumi's own uncertainty becomes the clarifying mechanism:**

> *Takumi hesitated. He knew what he meant — or thought he did. But standing here, with Iris watching him with that particular expression, he wasn't entirely sure how to finish the sentence.*

This surfaces the ambiguity naturally and invites the reader to try again without announcing that the input was unclear. Iris's expression does the work.

---

## GENERAL TONE RULES

- The world is internally consistent and takes itself seriously even when the situation is absurd
- Iris's embarrassment about transforming is never played for cheap comedy — it is character, not gag
- Takumi's earnestness is never mocked by the narrative — only occasionally by circumstances
- The supernatural layer is genuinely dangerous — stakes are real even when the tone is light
- Every scene has at least one thing that rewards a reader who is paying close attention
